## Description

You've got a photo you love and you want it sitting on clean white, ready to post. BorderMe does exactly that, and nothing else. Pick your photos, pick a border, pick a shape, save.

Your whole photo, never cropped. Instagram's feed shapes are fussy, and most tools chop the edges off to make a photo fit. BorderMe builds the canvas around your picture instead, so every pixel you shot is still there. The trade is a little more border on one pair of sides when your photo's shape doesn't match the canvas, and I think that's a fair price.

Exact shapes:
- Instagram Portrait, 4:5
- Instagram Tall, 3:4
- Instagram Landscape, 1.91:1
- Square, 1:1
- Original, which keeps your photo's own shape and simply adds the border

Full resolution. BorderMe never enlarges a photo, so a small image stays at its native size rather than going soft. Inside the app, big photos are kept sharp up to around 8192 pixels on the long edge, which is far more than Instagram shows. When you use BorderMe from the Share sheet in Photos, iOS gives extensions much less memory to work with, so those copies are capped at around 3072 pixels on the long edge. Open the app itself when you want the biggest files.

Four border colours: white, off-white, light grey and black. Three thicknesses: Small is 2%, Medium 5% and Large 8% of your photo's shorter edge, so they look right on any shape.

Batches of up to 50. Choose a whole afternoon's worth of photos, set the look once, and save them all in one go. You get a live preview as you change things, and you can flick through each photo before you save.

From the Share sheet. Select photos in the Photos app, tap Share, and choose BorderMe. It remembers your last settings, so a quick post is a few taps.

Your originals are never touched. BorderMe adds new copies to your library and leaves the first ones exactly as they were. It only asks for permission to add photos, never to read your library; you choose which photos to hand over each time.

Looks after your details. Copyright, camera, lens and capture date all carry across to the bordered copy, so it's still yours and still says so. Location is removed, because GPS coordinates are rarely something you meant to publish along with the picture. The Instagram shapes and Square convert to sRGB, which is what Instagram expects; Original keeps your photo's own colour profile.

No accounts, no network, no analytics, no ads. Your photos never leave your phone, and BorderMe collects nothing about you. It's free, and it's a small thing made with care.

If something breaks or a preset you want is missing, open an issue on GitHub and tell me about it. Thank you for trying it.
